# Physics 101 Chapters 4-6: Delivery Upgrade

Status: production-ready content and storyboard brief
Prepared: 19 August 2026
Scope: Chapters 4-6 only; no player, shell, media or release changes

## 1. Fixed curriculum and product contract

This package preserves the approved chapter titles, learning targets, misconceptions and six-beat learning arc in `physics-101-advanced-grade-4-plan.md`.

- Chapter 4 explains how friction between touching surfaces can help or hinder motion and how to make a fair surface comparison.
- Chapter 5 explains gravity as an Earth-object non-contact interaction and separates it from air resistance.
- Chapter 6 tests magnetic materials, attraction, repulsion and the observed effect of distance.
- Each chapter contains exactly 12 narration cues in four production sections of three cues.
- Each chapter contains exactly 20 test variants: slots 1-10, variants `a` and `b`.
- Difficulty comes from prediction, force modelling, evidence and fair-test reasoning, not advanced terminology.
- The chapters remain unavailable until their final media and QA evidence exist.

The curriculum anchor is [Australian Curriculum AC9S4U03](https://v9.australiancurriculum.edu.au/support-resources/background-information/science_teacher_background_information_AC9S4U03_E5): identify object-object forces and investigate frictional, gravitational and magnetic effects on motion.

## 2. Research-backed delivery decisions

The delivery should become more physical and directed, not more visually crowded.

1. **Signal the exact evidence.** A 2025 study with 119 children found that visual signalling can support recall and protect learners with attentional difficulties from unsignalled multimedia demands. Each cue therefore has one dominant target, and its border, arrow or spotlight persists for the entire spoken clause. Source: [The impact of visual cues on reducing cognitive load in interactive storybooks for children](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40472509/).
2. **Segment cause and evidence.** Experimental work on learning from animations found benefits from meaningful segmentation and temporal cueing. Each chapter repeats a setup-predict-run-hold-explain rhythm, with the result held before interpretation. Source: [Explaining the segmentation effect in learning from animations](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0360131511003411).
3. **Use meaningful pointing and tracing.** Research on children's instructional videos reports stronger learning when an on-screen agent points to or traces relevant content, particularly for static diagrams. A compact gloved hand or light trace may identify a contact patch, force path or measurement, then leave the evidence area. Source: [Adding pedagogical agents who point or trace to children's instructional videos](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0360131526000564).
4. **Synchronise captions with the audio timeline.** Captions must convey the prerecorded narration and meaningful sound information without covering the evidence. Source: [W3C Understanding Success Criterion 1.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG20/Understanding/captions-prerecorded).

These findings guide production; they do not justify decorative motion. The evidence target remains legible while the camera and unrelated objects stay quiet.

## 3. Delivery system

### Voice and pace

- Audition a warm, natural Australian or internationally clear teacher voice rather than automatically reusing the first three chapters' voice.
- Target 145-155 spoken words per minute, with slower delivery around measurements and misconception corrections.
- Use short prediction pauses and observation holds; do not fill every second with speech.
- Read labels exactly as displayed. Stress the source and receiver in force labels: "mat **on cart**" and "Earth **on feather**".
- Avoid cartoon acting, shouting and repeated praise. Curiosity should come from the experiment and reveal.

### Visual and camera grammar

- Use one tactile experiment stage per chapter with convincing depth, material response and lighting.
- Establish once, then use medium experiment framing and macro evidence close-ups.
- Lock the camera during fair comparisons; matched shots must preserve object size, gap, start line and viewpoint.
- Put measurements on the physical stage: ruler ticks, landing frame, stopping line and table values should connect to the observed event.
- Use colour plus labels and direction. Never rely on colour alone.
- Begin a visual signal no later than the named word and retain it until the narration moves to a new referent.
- During narration, create a meaningful evidence change approximately every 6-8 seconds, while preserving deliberate 2-4 second result holds.

### Four-section timing template

| Section | Approx. time | Three-cue job |
|---|---:|---|
| 1. Predict and demonstrate | 0-45 s | Pose one concrete prediction, run the hero event, reveal the first comparison. |
| 2. Model and explain | 45-90 s | Build the force model progressively, compare roles or conditions, reveal measured evidence. |
| 3. Transfer and investigate | 90-135 s | Protect the fair test, diagnose an error, transfer the model to a useful design. |
| 4. Misconception and exit | 135-180 s | Test the tempting claim, replace it with an evidence-based model, finish with a new prediction. |

## 4. Chapter 4 storyboard: Friction Grips

New terms: **friction**. `Fair comparison`, `entry speed` and `stopping distance` are reused inquiry language.

### Section 1: predict and demonstrate

| Cue | Phase | Visual target and synchronization |
|---|---|---|
| `friction-predict` | Prediction | Split view of identical shoes on ice and bitumen. A gloved pointer traces only the two contact patches; freeze for the learner's choice. |
| `friction-shoe-demo` | Hero demo | Matched sideways pushes, then readable slip trails. Hold final distances while each contact patch is circumscribed through the full explanation. |
| `friction-cart-demo` | Comparative demo | One cart enters tile and textured mat at equal speed. Ghost the common entry frame, then reveal two stopping flags together. |

### Section 2: model and explain

| Cue | Phase | Visual target and synchronization |
|---|---|---|
| `friction-model` | Force model | Macro wheel-mat contact. Build the orange `mat on cart` arrow on the cart only after the interaction is named; keep it visible through "not stored". |
| `friction-two-jobs` | Concept comparison | Return to runner and cart in a clean two-shot. Highlight grip for the runner and slowing for the cart one at a time. |
| `friction-distance-evidence` | Data reveal | Run three small multiples per surface, then construct the two distance rows. Delay the conclusion badge until all six values are visible. |

### Section 3: transfer and investigate

| Cue | Phase | Visual target and synchronization |
|---|---|---|
| `friction-fair-plan` | Fair plan | Lock cart, wheels, line and entry-speed gate with visible checkmarks; leave surface as the single swappable element. |
| `friction-variable-check` | Error diagnosis | Show heavy/hard and light/gentle trials. Red brackets remain around all three changed conditions until the narration names every possible cause. |
| `friction-transfer` | Transfer | Cutaway bicycle: brake pad contact, tyre-road contact, then axle. Use one active contact glow at a time; no ambient wheel spin during labels. |

### Section 4: misconception and exit

| Cue | Phase | Visual target and synchronization |
|---|---|---|
| `friction-misconception` | Misconception trial | Place "friction is always bad" beside a runner. Reduce grip and show the foot slipping backward; stamp the claim only after the evidence settles. |
| `friction-correction` | Correct model | Build a two-column evidence board: helps grip / hinders coasting. Each example physically docks beneath its measured evidence. |
| `friction-exit` | Exit prediction | Two wet-path sole trials in matched framing. Hold 70 cm and 24 cm beside the final shoes without revealing a choice badge. |

## 5. Chapter 5 storyboard: Gravity Pulls

New terms: **gravity**, **air resistance**, **vacuum**.

### Section 1: predict and demonstrate

| Cue | Phase | Visual target and synchronization |
|---|---|---|
| `gravity-predict` | Prediction | Ball and feather at one lit release bar. Show identical height and release icons, then pause on a two-part prediction: result and reason. |
| `gravity-air-demo` | Open-air demo | Synchronous release with a slow-motion replay. Trace air flow only around the feather after its lag becomes visible. |
| `gravity-vacuum-question` | Controlled change | Move the same rig into a transparent chamber. Animate the air gauge falling; keep objects, height and release bar visually locked. |

### Section 2: model and explain

| Cue | Phase | Visual target and synchronization |
|---|---|---|
| `gravity-earth-pull` | Gravity model | Add violet `Earth on ball` and `Earth on feather` arrows on the receiving objects. Maintain arrows in both air-condition thumbnails. |
| `gravity-air-resistance` | Second interaction | Macro feather view with upward air-resistance arrow opposing downward motion. Do not remove the gravity arrow when the second force appears. |
| `gravity-vacuum-demo` | Hero reveal | Drop both in vacuum and hold a shared landing frame for four seconds. Overlay the previous open-air landing ghost only after the result is inspectable. |

### Section 3: transfer and investigate

| Cue | Phase | Visual target and synchronization |
|---|---|---|
| `gravity-direction-model` | Earth-scale model | Orbit the camera once to reveal arrows from three locations pointing to Earth's centre, then lock while labels are read. |
| `gravity-fair-comparison` | Fair plan | Side-by-side air and vacuum checklists. Highlight same object, height and release before illuminating the one changed air condition. |
| `gravity-transfer` | Transfer | Helium balloon rises through a still frame. Keep violet gravity down while a separate upward air force grows; avoid implying upward motion means no gravity. |

### Section 4: misconception and exit

| Cue | Phase | Visual target and synchronization |
|---|---|---|
| `gravity-misconception` | Misconception trial | Put "gravity needs air" over the vacuum result. Replay the shared fall beneath it, then cross out only the claim, not the evidence. |
| `gravity-correction` | Correct model | Layered comparison: gravity present in both; air resistance present only in air. Reveal one row per spoken question. |
| `gravity-exit` | Exit prediction | Flat and crumpled versions of the same paper appear in air and vacuum lanes. Stop before release so the learner must apply both-force reasoning. |

## 6. Chapter 6 storyboard: Magnets Push And Pull

New terms: **pole**, **attraction**, **repulsion**.

### Section 1: predict and demonstrate

| Cue | Phase | Visual target and synchronization |
|---|---|---|
| `magnet-predict` | Prediction | Label steel, aluminium, copper and wood samples. Magnet stops behind a gap line; learner selects predicted movers before motion. |
| `magnet-material-demo` | Hero demo | Approach at constant speed with a visible gap. Follow only the steel paperclip as it moves; keep non-responsive samples and labels in frame. |
| `magnet-repeat-materials` | Evidence | Three matched runs per material collapse into a response table. Use "no visible movement" rather than claiming no interaction was measurable. |

### Section 2: model and explain

| Cue | Phase | Visual target and synchronization |
|---|---|---|
| `magnet-pole-model` | Pole model | Red north and blue south remain fixed on both magnets across all subsequent shots. The pointer traces the facing ends, then exits. |
| `magnet-attraction` | Attraction | North-south release in a locked shot. Add one inward magenta arrow to each receiving magnet and hold until the attraction clause ends. |
| `magnet-repulsion` | Repulsion | Repeat the same composition with north-north. Animate outward arrows first, motion second, then hold the final wider gap. |

### Section 3: transfer and investigate

| Cue | Phase | Visual target and synchronization |
|---|---|---|
| `magnet-distance-plan` | Fair plan | Physical ruler sets 1, 4 and 8 cm. Lock magnets, pole orientation, track and two-second timer; only the start marker moves. |
| `magnet-distance-data` | Data reveal | Show each run before its bar grows. Keep units explicit and label the last result "less than 1 cm" rather than zero. |
| `magnet-transfer` | Transfer | Transparent cabinet catch shows magnet, steel plate and closing gap. Compare a wide failed gap with a narrow reliable gap in matched views. |

### Section 4: misconception and exit

| Cue | Phase | Visual target and synchronization |
|---|---|---|
| `magnet-misconception` | Misconception trial | "Every metal" sits above labelled steel, aluminium and copper. Illuminate the two metal counterexamples for the whole rejecting clause. |
| `magnet-correction` | Correct model | Three evidence tiles appear sequentially: tested material, facing poles, measured gap. No decorative field particles or invisible-track imagery. |
| `magnet-exit` | Exit prediction | Equal-gap north-south, north-north and north-aluminium rigs wait in one locked frame. Do not run them before the learner predicts all three. |

## 7. Assessment blueprint

Each chapter's 20 variants produce one question from each of ten slots. Options are four plausible child models, `answer` is zero-based, and feedback states the causal reason or evidence limit.

| Slots | Chapter 4 | Chapter 5 | Chapter 6 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | Interaction/direction; useful friction | Earth-object interaction; gravity direction | Tested materials; non-contact evidence |
| 3-4 | Hindering role; stopping-distance evidence | Air resistance; vacuum evidence | Attraction; repulsion |
| 5-6 | Changed/measured variables; fair comparison | Fair comparison; floating misconception | Fair distance test; distance data |
| 7-8 | Repeats; force-arrow model | Gravity-air misconception; Earth-scale direction | All-metals misconception; pole comparison |
| 9-10 | Named misconception; design transfer | Shape/air transfer; integrated explanation | Cabinet transfer; integrated prediction |

No question requires Newtons, formulas, formal mass-versus-weight distinctions, field-line interpretation or material knowledge not demonstrated in its chapter.

## 8. Production and QA gates

1. Confirm narration duration from the selected voice; adjust pauses before changing scientific wording.
2. Create VTT captions directly from the final narration and keep cue boundaries aligned to the 12 visual targets.
3. Check that every force arrow is anchored on the receiving object and every non-contact scene retains a visible gap.
4. Run dense two-second and five-second contact-sheet reviews. No narration stretch may exceed eight seconds without a meaningful evidence, camera or state change.
5. Review the complete videos at normal speed for voice, caption, highlight and physical-event synchronization. Borders and arrows must persist until their spoken referent ends.
6. Verify all 60 test records, two variants per slot, four unique options, valid zero-based answers, precise feedback and no giveaway distractors.
7. Keep the three chapters unavailable until final MP4, audio, captions, timelines, posters, card art, player QA and Parent Cockpit persistence QA all pass.
